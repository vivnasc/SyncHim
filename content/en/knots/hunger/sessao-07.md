## Block B: What You Did

Look at what you did.

21 days ago, you thought the problem was your husband not giving you enough attention. You asked, charged, watched, and the more you asked, the more he pulled back, and the more your hunger grew. You were trapped in a cycle and called it a marriage.

Now you know the name of what moved you. You know hunger was born long before him, in a house where love came and went. You know no amount of his attention would ever fill a hole he didn't dig. And you know, above all, that you can begin to sate yourself.

Seeing it isn't dissolving it. But the woman who saw her own hunger can't unsee. She may slip, she may devour again on a weak night, but she can't go back to confusing hunger with love forever. And seeing is the first act of freedom.

## Block C: About Your Marriage

I won't promise you anything about your husband.

It may be that, as your hunger fades, he draws close on his own, without knowing why, simply feeling that it's become light again to be with you. It happens often: the man who retreated from a hunger returns to a sated woman. It may take months. It may be that you, without hunger distorting everything, discover things about the relationship that hunger was hiding from you.

Either path is yours now. For the first time, you choose from a woman who sustains herself, not from a starving child searching for a lap.

## Block E: The Next Knot

Now listen carefully, because this matters.

You crossed the Hunger. It was your dominant knot, the one at the front of everything. But knots live in layers, and there's almost always a second one beneath the first, hidden behind it, only becoming visible when the one above loosens.

With Hunger, the knot that usually sits beneath has a name: **Abandonment**.

It makes sense, if you think about it. Hunger is the fear of abandonment disguised as appetite. You devoured attention with urgency because, deep down, you were certain it would run out, that he would leave, that you'd be alone again as you were in childhood. Now that hunger fades, that older fear, the fear of being left, will begin to show its face. You'll notice that you sabotage when things are good. That you flee before they can leave you. That a part of you doesn't believe anyone stays.

That isn't a relapse. It's the next layer becoming visible now that you have eyes to see it.
