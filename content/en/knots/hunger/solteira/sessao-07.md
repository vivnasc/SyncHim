## Block B: What You Did

Look at what you did.

21 days ago, you thought the problem was that no one good showed up, or that the good ones weren't interested in you. You fell in love in three dates, gave everything too soon, and watched them cool off without understanding why. You were trapped in a cycle and called it bad luck.

Now you know the name of what moved you. You know hunger was born long before any of them, in a house where love came and went. You know your urgency scared the good ones and attracted the unavailable. And you know, above all, that you can begin to sate yourself.

Seeing it isn't dissolving it. But the woman who saw her own hunger can't unsee. She may slip, she may grab again on a weak night, but she can't go back to confusing hunger with love forever. And seeing is the first act of freedom.

## Block C: About the Love That's Coming

I won't promise you anything about when, or who.

What I can tell you is what usually happens when hunger fades: you start losing interest in the men who gave you the addictive hot-and-cold, because that addiction was the hunger, not love. You start noticing the available ones, the calm ones, the ones who used to seem boring. And when someone appears, you'll be able to let him approach at his own pace, without suffocating him with a hunger he didn't cause.

For the first time, you'll choose from a woman who sustains herself, not from a starving child searching for a lap.

## Block E: The Next Knot

Now listen carefully, because this matters.

You crossed the Hunger. It was your dominant knot, the one at the front of everything. But knots live in layers, and there's almost always a second one beneath the first, hidden behind it, only becoming visible when the one above loosens.

With Hunger, the knot that usually sits beneath has a name: **Abandonment**.

It makes sense, if you think about it. Hunger is the fear of abandonment disguised as appetite. You grabbed with urgency because, deep down, you were certain you'd end up alone again, as you did in childhood. Now that hunger fades, that older fear, the fear of being left, will begin to show its face. You'll notice that you sabotage relationships around the third month. That you flee before they can leave you. That a part of you doesn't believe anyone stays.

That isn't a relapse. It's the next layer becoming visible now that you have eyes to see it.
