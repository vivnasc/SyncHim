## THE 21 SITUATIONS

Scale: **Never (0)** / **Sometimes (1)** / **Often (2)** / **Always (3)**

### Hunger (q1-q3)

q1. When he takes time to reply, I review the conversation to figure out what I said wrong.

q2. When we're both silent, I feel the need to break it.

q3. My mood depends heavily on how he woke up.

### Grip (q4-q6)

q4. I know better than he does what he needs to do day to day.

q5. I've given up asking — I'd rather do it myself, it's faster.

q6. When he makes a decision without consulting me, I feel disrespected.

### Smallness (q7-q9)

q7. I feel he could have someone better than me.

q8. I swallow things that hurt me to avoid making a scene.

q9. When he compliments me, I have trouble believing it.

### Suspicion (q10-q12)

q10. I've checked his phone behind his back more than once.

q11. When he's running late, I imagine scenarios before he arrives.

q12. I believe most men, sooner or later, cheat.

### Rescuer (q13-q15)

q13. I see potential in him that he doesn't see in himself.

q14. I do things for him that he should do on his own.

q15. If he were fine without me, I'd feel less needed.

### Abandonment (q16-q18)

q16. I'm afraid, even in good moments, that he'll leave one day.

q17. I've created distance on purpose before he could.

q18. When he pulls away even a little, I'm already preparing for the end.

### Vanishing (q19-q21)

q19. I no longer know what I enjoy doing outside of being a wife or mother.

q20. I feel invisible inside my own home.

q21. I demand his attention, but I haven't given attention to myself in years.

### Descriptions (per knot)

#### If Hunger:

> You need him to fill you.
>
> You live in anxious fusion. You watch. You anticipate. Your mood rises and falls with the tone of his voice. When he pulls away a little, you go fetch him — and that fetching is exactly what pushes him further.
>
> It isn't love. It's hunger. And hunger isn't your fault — it has an origin. It came before your husband.
>
> Your marriage isn't dying because he went cold. It's dying because your hunger exhausted him.

#### If Grip:

> You manage everything.
>
> The house, the children, the finances, his schedule. You say it's because it has to be. Deep down, control is the only way you know to feel safe.
>
> He's stopped proposing. Deciding. Showing up. You've already decided for both of you.
>
> You aren't suffocating him because you're bad. You're suffocating him because no one ever taught you that you can trust without controlling.

#### If Smallness:

> You think yourself less.
>
> Less beautiful, less interesting, less than he deserves. You live to earn the right to stay. You do everything right. You swallow. You smile.
>
> And he loses respect for you in silence. He doesn't say. He just drifts.
>
> Men don't desire those who erase themselves to serve. It's not unjust — it's how systems work. You aren't being punished. You're being treated as the woman you present yourself to be.

#### If Suspicion:

> You expect betrayal.
>
> You watch. You read messages. You build scenarios. When he's late, you've already lived through three infidelities in your head before he arrives.
>
> He feels caged. Watched. Guilty of things he hasn't done. He drifts. You take that as proof.
>
> Your knot isn't his untrustworthiness. It's that you're carrying an old betrayal that hasn't yet been seen.

#### If Rescuer:

> You see him as a project.
>
> You want to save him from something. You love him for his potential, not his present. You do things for him that he should do alone. You call it love.
>
> It's the fear of losing him if he heals on his own.
>
> He feels infantilised. And infantilised men either rebel or surrender. Neither one loves you back the way you want.

#### If Abandonment:

> You fear he'll leave.
>
> Always. Even in good moments. By anticipating, you sabotage. You create distance before he can. You punish him for crimes he hasn't committed.
>
> When he finally drifts — exhausted — you say "I knew it."
>
> Your knot isn't the relationship. It's an old abandonment that still hurts, using this man as rehearsal for an ending you've already lived before.

#### If Vanishing:

> You've disappeared.
>
> Inside the relationship, inside the house, inside yourself. You no longer know what you enjoy outside of being a wife or mother. You feel transparent.
>
> You demand his attention. But you haven't given yourself attention in years.
>
> Men respond to what's alive. You asked him to see someone you stopped inhabiting.
