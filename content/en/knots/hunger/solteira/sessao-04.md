# SESSION 4, THE HUNGER: THE ORIGIN (single)

**Tier:** 1 or 2 | **Knot:** Hunger | **Context:** single | **Access:** 3 days after Session 3

---

## Block A, How Hunger Is Born

Your hunger wasn't born in your adult love life.

When it began in you, you were a child. And love, in that house, in that time, wasn't constant. It came and went. Present one day and absent the next. You never knew, waking up, whether you'd get warmth or coldness. And you learned, before you had words, to live in vigilance over the presence of others.

Hunger is born in girls who had **intermittent love**.

It might have been a mother who was affectionate when she was well and absent when she wasn't. You learned to read her mood each morning, and your safety depended on that daily calculation.

It might have been a father who appeared and disappeared. Who promised and didn't always follow through. Who was radiant with you one weekend and unavailable the next. You learned that masculine love is something you chase, never something you have.

It might have been a house with many children and little attention, where you had to compete to be seen. You learned that attention is scarce, and you must grab it when it appears because it may not come back.

It might have been real loss. A father who died, who left, a sick mother, a separation. You learned, too early, that the people who fill you can vanish from one moment to the next.

It doesn't matter which of these is yours. The result was the same: your system learned that **love is unstable, scarce, and must be chased**. And that without someone's presence, you're not safe.

As an adult, you reproduce this with every man. The hunger activates the moment someone gives you attention, because your system recognizes the old pattern: *here's someone who could fill me, I must make sure he doesn't leave*. And you begin to chase, before even knowing if he's worth the chase.

The adult man you chase is not the father who came and went. Not the intermittent mother. But your system doesn't distinguish. It reactivates the old hunger and pours it onto whoever appears.

## Block C, What You Just Touched

What you just touched has a name. It's called **anxious attachment**.

The word is technical. What it describes is simple: you learned, as a child, that love is unstable, and your system developed a strategy of constant vigilance and pursuit to never lose it.

Women with anxious attachment have a rare capacity: **they love with a depth few people know**. You feel everything at high voltage. When you love, you love whole. This intensity, in the right context, with the right person, with your system regulated, is a gift.

But in its current state, it burns. Because your intensity isn't a response to the real man in front of you. It's the discharge of an old hunger that was waiting for a target. You fall in love with the possibility of being filled, not with the actual person. That's why you fall so fast, before knowing them.

And here's the hardest part to hear: **as long as your hunger decides for you, you'll never choose well**. Hunger grabs the first one who gives attention, even if he's wrong, even if he's unavailable, even if something in you knows he's no good. Because hunger doesn't want the right man. It wants to be fed now.

The crossing of this knot is learning, slowly, in small doses, to **sustain the emptiness without filling it with the first one who appears**. To discover that you exist even when no one is looking at you. To build, inside you, the constant presence that no one gave you as a child.

When you become your own source of safety, the hunger fades. And for the first time, you can look at a man and ask: *does he serve me?* instead of: *will he fill me?*

## Block D, Practice for the Next 3 Days

**Sit with the Emptiness.**

For 3 days, you'll reserve 10 minutes per day to do something difficult: **stay alone with the emptiness, without filling it**.

Sit. No phone. No television. No music. No calling anyone. No scrolling. No reaching for anything that takes you away from yourself.

And notice what rises.

Probably discomfort. Maybe anxiety. Maybe the urgent desire to grab the phone and find someone, any stimulus to fill you. Maybe sadness. Maybe that old emptiness you've spent your life fleeing from.

**Stay with it.** Don't fill it. Don't solve it. Just stay.

10 minutes. It's much more than it sounds.

What you're training is the capacity to exist without external nourishment. Each time you sit with the emptiness without filling it, it loses a little of its power over you. And the hunger, without the panic of emptiness to fuel it, begins to fade.

Each night, write: *Today, in my 10 minutes with the emptiness, _____ rose. What I usually do to escape this is _____.*

In 3 days, Session 5 opens. We're going to see what your hunger is doing to your relationships.
