# SESSION 6, THE HUNGER: THE PRACTICE THAT LASTS (married)

**Tier:** 1 or 2 · **Access:** 3 days after Session 5

## Block A: The Truth About Dissolving Hunger

Your hunger isn't going to disappear.

You need to hear this clearly, because it's the part no one tells you, and it will save you years of feeling like a failure. The hunger you've carried since childhood doesn't die. No session, no practice, no man, no year of therapy will erase it. It will be with you always, to some degree.

What changes is not the existence of the hunger. It's **where it feeds**.

Today, your hunger feeds on your husband. On his attention, his tone, his presence, his response. And since no person can feed a hunger this deep, it stays half-starved, always asking for more, always watching for more. This is what exhausts your marriage.

The crossing is not killing the hunger. It's teaching it, slowly, over months and years, to **feed on you first**. To be sated by your own presence, your own life, your own ground, before turning to him. When the hunger is at least partly sated from within, what you ask of your husband stops being everything. It becomes companionship, not survival. And only then, when he stops being your only nourishment, can he come close again without fear of being devoured.

## Block B: How Hunger Will Tighten Again

I'll tell you exactly when hunger will tighten again, so you'll recognize it when it comes instead of mistaking it for love.

It will come on nights when he arrives late and the silence of the house grows too large. It will come when he's on his phone and you feel that old pull to be seen. It will come around your period, when the body is more open and raw. It will come after a good stretch, when everything is fine and yet something in you seeks confirmation. It will come when a friend tells you her husband surprised her and you feel, without wanting to, the comparison bite.

And when it comes, it will speak the same language as always:

*"He doesn't care about me like before."*
*"If he loved me, he'd notice I'm struggling."*
*"I just need him to give me attention and I'll be fine."*

Notice: it's the same voice from Session 3. Hunger doesn't invent new phrases. It repeats the old ones. And because you recognize the voice, you gain the power. Before, when it spoke, you obeyed, because you believed it was the truth. Now, when it speaks, you'll recognize: *this is the hunger, not reality*. And between recognizing and obeying, the entire space of your freedom opens.

## Block C: Your Maintenance Practice

Each knot has a practice that stays for life. The hunger's is this, and it has a name: **sate myself before asking**.

It works in three beats, and you'll use it forever, thousands of times, until it stops being a technique and becomes who you are.

**Beat 1, notice.** The instant you feel the pull, the urge to seek attention, to send the message, to ask "are you upset with me?", to provoke so you'll be noticed, stop. Just notice: *the hunger woke up*.

**Beat 2, turn inward.** Before you do anything toward him, ask yourself: *what am I trying to fill right now, and can I give it to myself first?* Sometimes the answer is rest. Sometimes it's actual food. Sometimes it's crying. Sometimes it's calling a friend. Sometimes it's just placing your hand on your own chest and breathing.

**Beat 3, only then approach.** After you've given yourself the first bite, if you still want his presence, approach. But now you approach sated, not starving. And the difference between those two states is the difference between a woman he wants to hold and a woman he wants to protect himself from.

You won't do this well at first. You'll forget, you'll ask first, you'll devour. That's fine. The practice isn't about never failing. It's about noticing sooner, each time.

## Block D: A Letter

Write, now, a letter to yourself. Not to the future. To the past, to the woman you were 18 days ago, when you clicked the link with hunger screaming and didn't even know it had a name.

What would you tell her now that you know it was hunger, and not a lack of his love?

You don't have to be kind or harsh. You have to be honest. Tell her what was seen in Sessions 3, 4, and 5. Tell her what still hurts. Tell her what changed. Tell her what frightened you to discover about the origin of this hunger.

This letter is saved. It will return in Session 7, in 3 days, for you to read as if for the first time.
