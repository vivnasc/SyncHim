# SESSION 3, THE HUNGER: HOW IT WORKS IN YOU

**Tier:** 1 or 2 · **Access:** 3 days after Session 2

## Block A, The Mechanics

Your hunger isn't weakness. It's an old pattern operating exactly as it learned to.

It works like this: you don't feel whole alone. There's a constant low emptiness you don't even name, and your system learned, long ago, that this emptiness is filled by the other person's presence. So when he's attentive, you breathe. When he pulls away, you suffocate.

It isn't love. It's hunger.

Hunger disguises itself as many things.

It disguises as **care**, *"I'm just worried about him."*

As **interest**, *"I want to know how his day was."*

As **love**, *"It's because I love him so much."*

As **jealousy**, *"He's acting strange, I can feel it."*

But you know, deep down, it's none of these. It's fear of being alone with yourself.

Notice the physical signs of hunger acting in you:, You feel relief when he replies to a message, and tightness in the chest when he takes time., You feel a need to break silence between you, as if silence were rejection., Your morning mood depends on his morning mood. If he woke up well, your day will be good. If he seemed off, your day becomes a constant watch., If he leaves without telling you where, you replay last night's conversation to figure out if he was upset., If he says he's having dinner with colleagues, you wonder which colleagues, whether there are women.

This isn't you being crazy. This is your system responding to an old hunger with the methods it learned in childhood.

## Block B, The Secret Language

Hunger speaks inside you. It has its own phrases, phrases that sound reasonable, sensible, that are the knot in disguise.

Some of hunger's phrases in you:

> *"If I don't ask, he tells me nothing."*

> *"I just want to know because I care."*

> *"It's not control, it's that I know how he is."*

> *"He doesn't notice on his own. I have to remind him."*

> *"If I'm not watching, he drifts further."*

> *"I deserve to know, don't I?"*

> *"Why do I always have to ask?"*

> *"He didn't mean it. I know. But it still hurts."*

Each of these sounds justified. Each is hunger sustaining itself.

You don't have to stop thinking them. You only have to begin seeing they are the hunger's, not yours. Hunger uses your voice to stay alive.

## Block C, Reflection

Write. Not for me. For yourself.

**Question:** When do you feel the hunger acting most strongly? At night? When he leaves? When he's quiet? When you see other women? Describe in 3-10 lines your most frequent situation of active hunger.

## Block D, Practice for the Next 3 Days

**Don't Initiate.**

For 3 days, don't initiate conversation with him first. Not a message. Not a call. Not a question at dinner. Not a comment in the kitchen.

This is **not punitive silence**. Not playing hard. Not a trick.

It's observation.

You'll notice something disturbing: the urge to initiate will appear 20, 30, 50 times across these 3 days. Each time, notice, not what you wanted to say to him, but what you were feeling before wanting to speak.

Were you thirsty for presence? Anxious? Feeling invisible? Afraid he was upset with you?

The urge to initiate is the sound of hunger asking for food. In these 3 days, you'll hear that sound for the first time without feeding it immediately.

**Rules:**

- If he initiates, reply normally. Don't withdraw.
- Practical or urgent matters (kids, household, logistics), communicate.
- If he asks if everything's okay, say "yes, it is."
- Before bed, write down: *How many times today did I want to initiate? What was I feeling before?*
