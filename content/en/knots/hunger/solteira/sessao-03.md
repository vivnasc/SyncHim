# SESSION 3, THE HUNGER: HOW IT WORKS IN YOU (single)

**Tier:** 1 or 2 | **Knot:** Hunger | **Context:** single | **Access:** 3 days after Session 2

---

## Block A, The Mechanics

Your hunger isn't love. It isn't "having a big heart." It's an old system that learned, long ago, that someone's presence is what keeps you standing.

It works like this: you meet a man, and in a short time he occupies a space inside you larger than he even knows exists. You read his messages searching for signs. You replay conversations to figure out if he liked you. When he takes long to reply, you've already traveled through three scenarios before his answer arrives. When he replies quickly, you breathe. When he disappears for a day, you suffocate.

You don't control this. It's not a choice. It's a hunger that activates the moment someone gives you attention, and it grows with every drop you receive, never sated, always asking for more.

That's why you grab fast. Give too much, too soon. Make yourself entirely available before he's shown he deserves it. Cancel your own plans to be free. Reply in seconds. Show up always. And all of this, which you feel as generous surrender, arrives to the other person as **weight**.

They feel the hunger. They can't name it, but their system detects it: *this woman needs something from me that I don't know if I can give*. And they pull back. Not from cruelty. From reflex. Men withdraw from hungers they didn't know they provoked.

And each withdrawal confirms your fear. *"See? Once again I wasn't enough."* And the fear makes you grab even sooner with the next one. And the next one pulls back even faster. The hunger feeds the cycle the hunger fears.

Notice the signs of hunger acting in you:

You know how long he took to reply to your last message.

You've already cancelled something of yours to stay available in case he called.

You fall in love before the fourth date. Always.

When you like someone, you lose your appetite, your sleep, your focus.

You check your phone many times an hour when you're waiting for his message.

You've humiliated yourself, sent too many messages, called too many times, for someone who wasn't reciprocating.

Alone, you feel an emptiness that only a man's attention seems to fill.

This isn't you being weak. It's an old child with hunger using each new man as possible nourishment.

## Block B, The Secret Language

Hunger speaks in you in phrases that sound like romance, passion, beautiful intensity. Each one is the knot in disguise.

> *"I love intensely. It's my nature."*

> *"When I like someone, I give myself completely."*

> *"I just want someone who loves me the way I love."*

> *"If he really liked me, he'd reply right away."*

> *"I feel like he's the one."*

> *"I can't do things halfway."*

> *"I deserve someone who gives me the attention I give."*

> *"I'm too much for most men."*

Each has a foot in truth. That's what makes them poisonous. Hunger never lies completely. It mixes a legitimate desire for love with an old need, and you believe you're being passionate when you're being famished.

The difference between loving and hungering is simple: love can wait. Hunger has urgency. Love keeps you whole while it surrenders. Hunger dissolves you into the other.

## Block C, Reflection

Write. Not for me. For yourself.

**Question:** Think of the last 2 or 3 men you were interested in. How long before you were "hooked"? And what did you feel in your body during the days you waited for their reply? Describe, in 5-10 lines, that physical sensation of waiting. Don't soften it.

## Block D, Practice for the Next 3 Days

**Don't Reply First.**

For 3 days, with any man you're interested in (or if there's no one now, with any person whose attention you tend to chase), you'll practise **not being the one to initiate contact**.

The rule: if the impulse comes to send a message, call, "check how he's doing," comment on something of his, **stop**. Don't act. Notice the impulse. Feel it in your body. And don't act.

If he writes to you, you can reply, but without the old urgency. Let time pass. Don't reply in seconds. Don't expand into three paragraphs what fits in one line.

If there's no one right now, the practice is with your phone in general: notice how many times you seek stimulation, validation, attention outside yourself. And don't give in to every impulse.

Physical anxiety will appear. The emptiness will scream. You'll feel that if you don't act, you'll lose the opportunity, he'll forget you, someone else will appear. **None of that is true.** It's the hunger panicking at not being fed.

Each night, write: *Today I felt the impulse to chase attention _____ times. I acted _____ times. What I felt by not acting was _____.*

In 3 days, Session 4 opens. We're going to descend to the origin of this hunger.
